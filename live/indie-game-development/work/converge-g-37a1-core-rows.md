## §ROWS — seventeen, one per core line

Each row states: **OBSERVABLE** (what must be true in the world), **OUTSIDER** (how a person who
did not write it decides yes or no), **EVIDENCE** (where the decision is made), **FORCES** (the
parameters the row's mechanism forces into existence — born here, not at task time), and marks:
`BUILD` = a build closes this better than a document; `SETTING Kn` = carries a registered knob;
`OWNER` = cannot be decided without him.

### A. The man and the launch

**ROW 1 — the game starts as a game.** (line 1 · criterion 1) `BUILD`

- **OBSERVABLE.** A named file exists. Double-clicking it on a machine that has never had Unity
  installed opens the game. Closing it and double-clicking again opens it again, in the same
  starting condition. No Editor, no console, no manual repair of any file.
- **OUTSIDER.** Hand the artifact to a person who has never opened Unity, say nothing but "start
  it, then start it again". PASS if both starts reach a state where a character responds to input.
  FAIL if either start needs a terminal, an editor, a file edit or a developer.
- **EVIDENCE.** The packaged player, run on a second physical machine with no Unity.
- **FORCES.** Which scenes the player contains; where the parameter home lives relative to the
  executable (couples to row 5); what "the same starting condition" means — which is the run
  lifecycle, and it is not answered anywhere → §OWNER O-B.
- **HIGHEST LATE-DISCOVERY RISK IN THE SET.** The first clause of the card is the one with no
  price and no plan: `EditorBuildSettings` holds exactly one enabled scene (the empty URP template),
  nine real scenes exist and none is in the list, there is zero build automation anywhere in the
  product and zero player-build mention across 53 ADRs, and a gate receipt records the empty default
  as a deliberately preserved invariant. This needs no owner decision — criterion 1 already IS his
  answer — it needs a named task with a price.
  Receipt: `history/2026-07-27-s-converge-verify-g-37a1-digging-card-001.md` V6.

**ROW 2 — the body.** (line 2 · criterion 3) `BUILD`

- **OBSERVABLE.** A controlled character walks on ground, jumps, leaves the ground and comes back
  down under gravity, stands on a surface, and cannot pass through rock or fall through the floor.
- **OUTSIDER.** Give the controls to someone who did not build it, instruction "walk, jump". PASS
  if they cross flat ground, jump onto a step and stand on it, jump into a hole and land at its
  bottom, and cannot walk through a wall. FAIL on any of: sliding through geometry, floating, no
  fall, a jump that never lands.
- **EVIDENCE.** The built player of row 1.
- **FORCES.** Walk speed; jump height; the height of a step the body mounts without jumping; fall
  behaviour; and the one that couples the body to everything else — **the body's size relative to
  the smallest diggable unit**, because it decides whether a single cut makes a hole a man fits in.
- **THIS ROW IS NEW WORK, NOT A CHECK.** The body in the product today integrates a strictly planar
  walk with Y written once at seed time and never again; there is no gravity, no ground contact and
  no collider read by movement code. Receipt: same, V7.

**ROW 3 — he gets back up himself.** (line 3 · criterion 3) `BUILD` `SETTING K5`

- **OBSERVABLE.** From the bottom of a shaft he cut himself, a route to the surface can be made
  using only the dig verb and the jump. The game contains no ladder, rope, lift, grapple, teleport
  or return-to-surface shortcut that substitutes for it. A player who cuts a shaft he cannot mount
  and does not cut steps is stuck, and being stuck resolves as a death (row 4), never as a freeze.
- **OUTSIDER.** (a) Ask an outsider to dig down at least three of their own jump-heights and get
  back. PASS if they can, using only digging and jumping, without being taught a technique. (b) Ask
  an outsider to make themselves unable to get out. PASS if they can, and if the game then ends the
  attempt as a death rather than leaving them alive and stuck.
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player.
- **FORCES.** Jump height against mountable step height (**K5**, «насколько крутые уклоны и
  перепады»); whether the dig verb can cut the surface under the body; whether the body may dig
  while airborne. The value of K5 is NOT decided here — the row tests that the knob exists and that
  changing it changes how far a player can climb.

**ROW 4 — death costs zero and returns you at once.** (line 4 · criterion 9) `SETTING K2` `OWNER O-B`

- **OBSERVABLE.** A death state exists and is reachable. On death the player is back in control
  immediately. Nothing he had is taken from him at the default value.
- **OUTSIDER.** Ask an outsider to die twice and report what it cost. PASS if they say "nothing"
  both times and are playing again within the time it takes to say it; FAIL if a menu, a load
  screen, a lost item or a lost position is reported. Second half: raise K2 from the parameter home
  without a rebuild and repeat; PASS if the outsider now reports that death costs something.
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player plus the parameter home.
- **FORCES.** What causes death (air at zero, row 15; being sealed in, row 3; anything else is not
  in the seventeen lines and must not be added silently); **where the player returns**; and **what
  the world looks like after his death** — neither is answered anywhere → §OWNER O-B.
- **DERIVED CONSEQUENCE, named because it is not obvious.** Nothing in the seventeen lines refills
  air. At the default K2 = 0, **death is therefore the cheapest air refill in the game.** That is
  not an argument against zero — it is precisely why K2 is a knob and why O-B's return point
  matters, because a return at the entrance charges the walk back in air and a return in place does
  not charge anything at all.

**ROW 5 — every contested number is outside, and you can restart and compare.** (line 5 · criterion 12) `BUILD`

- **OBSERVABLE.** Every number in §KNOBS is readable and writable from the BUILT player, without
  Unity, without a code edit and without a rebuild. Changing one and restarting produces a run in
  which the change is visible. The same section is re-entered, so two runs are comparable.
- **OUTSIDER.** Give an outsider the parameter home and one instruction: "make the substance come
  out faster", then "make air run out sooner", then "make the ground harder to read". PASS if they
  find the knob, change it, restart, and describe a difference, with no developer, no code editor
  and no Editor. **A knob whose change produces no describable difference FAILS as a knob even
  though the file accepted the value.**
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player, the parameter home, and the §KNOBS list committed to the repository
  BEFORE the sitting — so the list cannot be trimmed afterwards to whatever happened to work.
- **FORCES.** Where the parameter home lives (a file beside the executable / an in-game console / a
  start-up screen); whether a change needs a restart or takes effect live; whether values are
  validated — an out-of-range value must fail loudly, because a silent clamp makes every comparison
  a lie.
- **HONEST LIMIT, stated rather than promised.** The comparison instrument is the owner's eye on a
  FIXED section (row 12 fixes the section before the build). It is not numeric and it is not
  reproducible input-for-input. Criterion 12 asks for «менять условия, перезапускать и сравнивать
  результат» and this delivers exactly that, no more.

### B. The verb

**ROW 6 — digging: you cut where you decided, and rock costs time.** (line 6 · criteria 2, 5) `BUILD`

- **OBSERVABLE.** The player removes rock at a place he chose; removing it takes time he can feel;
  the removed rock is gone and the space is open; nothing in the level restricts WHERE he may cut.
- **OUTSIDER.** (a) Give the same section to two outsiders separately and ask each to reach the
  bottom. PASS if the two routes cut in different places. (b) Ask an outsider to reach the bottom
  **without breaching any pocket**. PASS if it is possible — which is what proves there is no
  authored breach panel — and if it visibly costs more time than a route through one. FAIL if the
  game refuses a cut somewhere, or if both routes are forced through the same faces.
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player; two recorded routes.
- **FORCES.** What one cut removes and at what granularity relative to the substance unit; the cut
  rate (time per unit); reach; whether a cut can be interrupted; and the feedback contract —
  **legible progress, predictable duration, continuous sound, a discrete end** (the four-part break
  contract, all free at zero art; `work/converge-g-37a1.md` §12, Minecraft).
- **NOTE on criterion 2's negative half.** «не заранее расставленной автором панели» is a property
  of the LEVEL, not of the verb, and test (b) is the only thing in this set that decides it.

**ROW 7 — the value is below; they dig down and sideways.** (line 7 · criterion 3) `OWNER O-A`

- **OBSERVABLE.** *(none that can be written without him — see §OWNER O-A).* What is checkable
  without him and is checked: the section extends downward; downward digging is possible; the body
  descends into its own workings and returns (rows 2, 3).
- **OUTSIDER.** Only a MEASUREMENT is legal here, not a pass rule: observe an uninstructed outsider
  for one session and record whether he goes down. Writing a pass rule for «ценность внизу» before
  the owner says what is below would be inventing the value the whole route exists to avoid.
- **THIS IS THE ONE ROW OF SEVENTEEN WITH NO OBSERVABLE.** Under the rest of the set, going down is
  strictly worse than digging sideways: the way back is longer and what you release rises into the
  shaft you cut. → §OWNER O-A.

**ROW 8 — what is dug is not filled back in.** (line 8 · criterion 10)

- **OBSERVABLE.** No action returns rock to a place it was removed from and no action reduces the
  open volume. Within a run, open volume is monotone non-decreasing.
- **OUTSIDER.** Ask an outsider to fill a hole they made and to report whether they can. PASS if
  the answer is no and nothing in the controls or the world offers it.
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player plus the control list.
- **FORCES.** Nothing new. Its CONSEQUENCE is load-bearing: with row 11 the workings grow
  monotonically while the substance spreads monotonically, so **a section is consumed by play and
  has a natural end state** — which is one of the three reasons §OWNER O-B must be answered.
- A document closes this row; a build confirms it in a minute.

### C. The substance

**ROW 9 — the breach is an event, and its concrete form is a setting.** (line 9 · criterion 6) `BUILD` `SETTING K1`

- **OBSERVABLE.** At the moment a pocket is opened, something HAPPENS that a watcher can point at —
  a state with an onset and an end — rather than a number quietly changing. Two breaches of the
  same pocket, one through a wide cut and one through a narrow notch, differ visibly: the wide one
  is stronger and faster; the narrow one is a thin trickle. A player standing at the face when his
  first breach happens is still alive seconds later.
- **OUTSIDER.** (a) Shuffled 3–4 s clips of a wide breach and a narrow breach; the outsider says
  which released more, and faster. PASS if he orders them correctly and consistently without being
  told what to look for. (b) An outsider makes his first breach standing at the face, on the
  DEFAULT knob values, pass rule committed before the sitting. PASS if he is alive long enough to
  move away.
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player plus a committed clip deck.
- **SETTING GUARD.** **K1** — what exactly happens (force, speed, accumulated pressure, ramp) — is
  NOT decided here. The row tests four things and no more: an event exists; aperture width is one
  of its inputs; the event's form is selectable from the parameter home; and at least one selectable
  form satisfies "the first breach does not kill instantly".
- **FORCES.** The event's inputs (aperture area, pocket contents, time since the cut); whether it
  has a fixed duration or ends on a condition; whether it is one event per breach or repeats as the
  aperture widens.
- **COUPLING FINDING, not previously named anywhere.** **K1 and K3 are not independent.** «Первое
  вскрытие не убивает мгновенно» is a property of the PAIR — a violent breach form combined with a
  large per-kind air-rate gap can kill instantly with both knobs inside their own ranges. Either K3
  carries an upper bound, or the rig must demonstrate the property across K3's range. Nothing in
  state says which. This is a constraint on the shape leg, not a value to pick here.
- The record names this the most expensive line in the set for the existing simulation; its price is
  step 4's answer, not this leg's.

**ROW 10 — the substance moves by a law, and nothing in that motion is computed from the player.** (line 10 · criterion 4)

- **OBSERVABLE.** The rule that moves the substance is stated in advance, holds everywhere in the
  section, and takes no input naming the player: not his position, not his distance, not his facing,
  not which space he is in. The substance behaves the same whether he watches it or walks away.
- **OUTSIDER.** (a) Reading test: give a person who did not write it the stated rule and the
  section, and have them predict where the substance will be after some time. PASS if the prediction
  is right in KIND (which way it went), not in number. (b) Behaviour test: breach, leave the area,
  return; PASS if the substance is where the rule says. (c) List test: PASS if no knob in §KNOBS
  names the player in its name or its effect.
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player, the stated rule, the §KNOBS list.
- **FORCES.** What the rule's inputs actually are → HOW, `PLAN`/step 4. Whether a director may
  change inputs at runtime — «may a rule decide WHERE substance can appear while the crew is already
  digging?» — is ALREADY OPEN and routed to `i-substance-laws-canon-candidate`; it is named here and
  deliberately not reopened.
- A document plus the knob list closes the ban; only a build closes how the law FEELS.

**ROW 11 — nothing disappears; what left the pocket is in your workings; there are no sinks.** (line 11 · criterion 10)

- **OBSERVABLE.** The total substance in the section is the same after a breach as before it. What
  left a pocket is somewhere in the space the crew has cut. No action, object, place or timer
  removes substance from the section.
- **OUTSIDER.** (a) Breach a pocket, wait, report whether the workings clear. PASS if they never do.
  (b) Ask the outsider to find any way to get rid of the substance. PASS if there is none. (c) A
  total-mass readout is equal at the start and the end of a session — instrumented, but reproducible
  by anyone with the readout.
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player plus a total readout exposed in the parameter home / debug channel.
- **FORCES.** Whether the section boundary is closed (nothing leaks out of the box); whether the
  substance can occupy un-dug rock or only open space — the parked cell-versus-column question,
  which decides WHERE displaced substance actually goes. Technical, routed, not decided here.
- **THIS ROW IS WHAT CLOSES «WAITING IS ALWAYS OPTIMAL», and the check is one line:** after a breach
  there is no state to wait FOR, because the substance does not leave. Test: wait five minutes; the
  situation is not better. This is the direction's oldest unfixed problem and it closes with no new
  mechanic (`work/converge-g-37a1.md` §11.2, whose own banner records that its two prior cures are
  dead).
- **DERIVED ACCEPTANCE PROPERTY, and it is the strongest loop in the concept.** Rows 3 + 11 together:
  a light kind released at the bottom of a shaft rises into the very steps you cut to get out. It
  is a CONSEQUENCE of two rows, not a new mechanic, and it is testable — cut a shaft, breach a light
  pocket at its base, confirm the climb becomes expensive.

**ROW 12 — a finite total, fixed before the build.** (line 12 · criterion 10)

- **OBSERVABLE.** The amount of substance and the dimensions of the section are set before the
  player is built and do not increase during a run. No source adds substance while playing.
- **OUTSIDER.** The total readout at the start equals the total at any later point (with row 11 this
  is one test); and §KNOBS shows the total as a pre-run value, never as a rate.
- **EVIDENCE.** Parameter home plus the total readout.
- **FORCES.** How many pockets, of what sizes, where, and of which kind — level-authoring inputs,
  explicitly NOT generation (criterion 12: an experiment instrument, not a level generator).
- **CONFLICT NAMED, NOT REPAIRED.** The record establishes first-hand that «sources are finite» has
  no implementation anywhere in the product and that the only shipped authored source is INFINITE by
  owner-signed default — so this row fails by construction on today's primitives. Confirming or
  refuting that is step 4's job. Receipt: `history/2026-07-27-s-converge-verify-g-37a1-digging-card-001.md` V3.

**ROW 13 — any pocket may be entered; the difference is the price in air; the kinds are told apart by eye.** (line 13 · criterion 7) `BUILD` `SETTING K3`

- **OBSERVABLE.** (a) No pocket refuses entry. (b) The same time spent in kind A and in kind B costs
  visibly different amounts of air. (c) A pocket whose substance has left costs nothing to be in.
  (d) An outsider can tell the two kinds apart by looking — no instrument, no on-screen readout —
  from OUTSIDE the pocket, before entering it.
- **OUTSIDER.** (a) Ask an outsider to walk into every pocket he finds; PASS if none blocks him.
  (c) Ask him to re-enter one he emptied; PASS if he reports it now costs nothing. (d) **The
  legibility deck, EXTENDED.** The existing instrument (`work/converge-g-37a1.md` §14) is the
  strongest verification tool in the record but it measures NAMING CONSISTENCY only, and its own
  banner says it cannot test «это видно до того, как в них вошли». So the deck gains a second half:
  shuffled stills and 3–4 s clips taken from OUTSIDE a pocket; the outsider names the kind for each
  frame; both pass rules committed before the sitting. FAIL if two kinds get the same word or one
  kind gets two words within the sitting (§14's rule, unchanged), AND FAIL if the outsider cannot
  beat chance on frames shot from outside (the new half). Under the game's own light condition, not
  studio lighting.
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player plus a committed frame deck.
- **SETTING GUARD.** **K3** — how large the air-rate gap is — is not decided here. The row tests
  that a gap exists, that it is a knob, and that at the default an outsider describes the difference
  unprompted.
- **FORCES.** Which visual channel carries the difference — extinction distance, emission versus
  absorption, a hard straight interface, colour last; two kinds is the honest ceiling
  (`work/converge-g-37a1.md` §10, cheapest legibility at zero art).
- **KNOWN PLACE WHERE (b) AND (d) CAN DISAGREE, named and not fixed.** The shipped render payload
  carries the mixture's total density plus the DOMINANT kind per cell, so a minority kind is
  unrepresentable while it is still charging air — exactly in the two situations the design makes
  central: the first trace escaping a fresh notch, and any region where the two kinds have met. The
  cheap resolution already named in product code is single-type-per-region seeding, a build-time
  choice. Receipt: same, V5.

**ROW 14 — the rock reads: before the cut you see there is a pocket and roughly how big.** (line 14 · criterion 2) `BUILD` `SETTING K4`

- **OBSERVABLE.** A rock face with a pocket behind it looks different from a face with nothing
  behind it, and the difference GRADES with the pocket's size. The mark holds and releases nothing —
  it is a mark, not a store, or it becomes a sink and breaks row 11 (R17).
- **OUTSIDER.** Show an outsider pairs of rock faces: which has a pocket behind it, and which pocket
  is bigger. Pass rules committed before the sitting. PASS if he beats chance on BOTH questions.
  **FAIL if the mark signals presence only and not size** — because then the breach is a coin toss
  on size and the trade cannot be priced at all.
- **EVIDENCE.** Built player plus committed frame pairs.
- **SETTING GUARD.** **K4** — how far ahead and how sharply — is a knob, not a value decided here.
- **FORCES.** The channel (a stain in the rock; or sound while digging — material-distinct sound
  carries more than graphics at zero art, §12); the range at which it reads; whether it reads
  through more than one cut-depth of rock.

## §SEAM — where the excluded things touch the modelled thing

Criterion 13 excludes by name. What it does not say is what happens at the boundary, which is the
thing that forces an unbudgeted decision mid-build. Four seams exist and all four are decidable now.

1. **A held object inside the substance.** Picking an object up is permitted; substance-versus-loose-object
   physics is cut from the simulation. **Seam: nothing happens.** The object is unaffected and does
   not affect the substance. Checkable: carry an object into a pocket; neither changes.
2. **Two kinds meeting in the same space.** Reactions are excluded. **Seam: they mix and nothing
   else.** Checkable: join two pockets and confirm no event of any kind fires. This also removes the
   entire firing-threshold apparatus of the previous converge's §8 from the first core.
3. **A shaft too steep to leave.** Ready ladders and lifts are excluded. **Seam: this is the
   legitimate death of criterion 3**, not a soft-lock — row 3 test (b).
4. **Liquid.** Excluded from the first core, kept as headroom, and possibly a REPLACEMENT for gas
   rather than an addition. **Seam: none in this outcome, and its price is owed the day it is
   admitted** (`i-substance-states-unpriced`, which stays open for exactly that reason).

## §OWNER — rows that cannot be decided without him. Options and a recommendation. NOT decided.

Batched, per G7. None of these is a contested NUMBER — a contested number becomes a knob and is in
§KNOBS. These are three questions where every possible answer changes what the game IS, so a leg
without him may not pick one.

### O-A — What is below?

**Why it matters.** Criterion 3 says «Ценность внизу: копают вниз и вбок», and nothing in the
seventeen lines gives a player anything he wants down there. Worse, under the rest of the set going
down is strictly WORSE than digging sideways near the surface: the way back is longer, and what you
release rises into the shaft you cut. Row 7 is the one row of seventeen with no observable, and an
uninstructed playtester will dig sideways.

- **(a) The section simply ENDS below** — one authored destination at the bottom; reaching it ends
  the attempt. *Downside:* it authors a DESTINATION, which will be misheard by someone as authoring
  the route (it is not — it fixes where you are going, never where you cut). *Upside:* costs
  nothing, needs no new mechanic, matches `g-5e8c`'s «прошли / не прошли», and makes depth the price
  of the run.
- **(b) Pockets get bigger and denser deeper** — free space and risk both scale with depth.
  *Downside:* free space is only valuable if space is scarce, and in the first core it is not; it
  makes the deep game the dangerous game with no matching reward.
- **(c) Something to collect is placed below.** Loot is not banned. *Downside:* it adds a carrying or
  economy surface the core deliberately excludes, and R26's profit rule has never been run on it.
- **(d) Leave it as level-authoring direction, not a mechanic** — the first core simply has more
  section below the entrance. *Downside:* row 7 stays undecidable and the playtest will show
  sideways play.

**Recommendation: (a).** It is the cheapest of the four, it authors not one cut, it turns depth into
the price of a run, and it is the only one that makes rows 3, 11 and 15 bite together — the deeper
you are, the longer the way back and the more of what you released sits between you and the surface.

### O-B — What is a run, and what survives a death and a relaunch?

**Why it matters, and it is load-bearing in four rows.** Row 4 returns him at once — WHERE? Row 1
promises a repeat launch — into WHAT? Rows 8 and 11 make the section monotone, so a section is
consumed by play: if it persists, day two starts in day one's wreckage and criterion 14's «играет в
разные дни» quietly becomes one long game; if it resets, then the legitimate death of criterion 3
costs only the walk back — and at the default price of zero, dying becomes the cheapest way to refill
air, because nothing else refills it.

- **(a) A run is one descent; death ends it; the section resets; every launch is a fresh section.**
  *Downside:* nothing you built survives, so the workings never become a place. *Upside:* every day
  is comparable, which is what row 5's rig needs in order to mean anything.
- **(b) The section persists across deaths inside one session and resets on relaunch; he returns at
  the entrance.** *Downside:* suicide-to-refill remains a live strategy, priced only by the walk
  back. *Upside:* the workings become a place he knows, which is the closest thing the core has to
  progress, while the rig keeps its comparability.
- **(c) The section persists across launches.** *Downside:* it silently turns the first core into a
  save-game feature, makes the rig's comparison meaningless, and is the most expensive of the three.

**Recommendation: (b).** It keeps the comparability the rig needs, it makes the walk back the real
price of a death — so K2 = 0 is honest rather than exploitable — and it is the smallest change.
**Stated so it is not mistaken for his verdict:** his recorded words on death, «игрок возвращается
назад, всё, он сразу готов», are compatible with (b) but do not settle where «назад» is, and this
leg refuses to read a location into them.

### O-C — What operationally ends this outcome?

**Why it matters.** Criterion 14 forbids the section becoming «вечной лабораторией» — the deliberate
guard against the `g-12fd` failure, which burned three legs and eighteen findings and produced no
product. The phrase appears once in the whole record and is defined nowhere, so the guard is
currently unenforceable, and its own criterion says his verdict decides what to do NEXT rather than
whether the result is achieved.

- **(a) A number of distinct play-days after which `review` runs whether or not a verdict exists.**
  *Downside:* a date-like trigger, and he has just removed dates as conditions. *Upside:* it is the
  only form that actually stops a laboratory.
- **(b) Closure is triggered by the first day work starts on the next node.** *Downside:* it makes
  the guard depend on unrelated scheduling. *Upside:* no new number.
- **(c) Leave it as prose.** *Downside:* the guard does nothing, which is how `g-12fd` ran.

**Recommendation: (a)** with a small count he sets himself. This is a PROCESS number, not a game
number, so the settings rule does not apply and it cannot be deferred to the rig.
